# Evaluation and claim semantics

Evaluation turns production failures into reproducible evidence and then into a
deterministic verifier wherever possible. A fixture proves only the claims its evidence
actually covers.

## Gate statuses

- `eligible` means every required contract and evidence claim passed.
- `incomplete` means the input is valid but required evidence or a solution is absent.
- `rejected` means an input, assertion, proposal, or result violates a contract or policy.

An exception is not a fourth status. No retry, fallback, adjacent asset, or model opinion
may silently convert an incomplete or rejected result into success.

## Evidence levels

Evidence is ordered:

```text
declared < measured < simulated < engine_verified
```

Requirements name their minimum level. A multiview caption can suggest that an object is
a chair, but it cannot prove collision, a usable seat, an interface, a navigable room, or
a successful Studio import. `engine_verified` requires creator-run readback bound to the
exact world and observed-state hashes.

## Catalog search smoke evidence

`registry/catalog-search-smoke.json` contains 32 exact source identities spanning whole
objects, structural and route pieces, attachments, aggregates, collision companions,
organic/environment assets, style contrasts, out-of-kernel nouns, and prior render/name
failures. It checks schema plumbing, provenance, cache reuse, deterministic indexing, and
representative examples.

The smoke fixture cannot unlock corpus enrichment, select a production model, or support a
catalog-admission claim. Its earlier 8B/32B observations are retained as exploratory
evidence only.

## Production catalog qualification

Production qualification is currently an authoring sequence, not an executable benchmark. The
filename-derived 192-asset fixture and its thresholds were retired rather than treated as gold
data. Leakage groups still cannot cross development, model-selection, and holdout splits.

The bounded first stage contains 24 development assets. Internal multi-valued tags select varied
visual-composition, source-role, completeness, semantic-boundary, source-name, style, and
vegetation cases. Those tags are unscored proposal metadata and never enter the creator decision.
The reviewer answers one question per asset—whether the proposed text accurately describes what is
visible—using `accurate`, `edited`, `unsure`, or `image_problem`. That decision does not approve
contextual use, assembly purpose, completeness, query text, relevance, or validator behavior.

Only after the description pilot is independently reviewed and judged useful may the next stages
author distinct realistic requests, form union pools from every retrieval method being compared,
and independently judge query–asset relevance. The development query set contains no answer key:
its coverage tags select appearance, metric, whole/component, close-alternative, aggregate,
no-match-probe, style, and unobservable-property cases without asserting their outcomes. Mixed
pools keep background results judgeable; unresolved judgments remain unknown. Validator cases are
then authored from those exact reviewed queries and candidates and receive a separate independent
verdict review.

The current development pool binds 20 requests to 230 query–asset pairs contributed by basename,
hybrid, image-text, and metadata retrieval. It contains 24 reviewed-cohort candidates and 82
distinct background candidates. The search index explicitly excludes 61 measured sources whose
parts are all non-renderable collision proxies; those exclusions are sealed coverage, not missing
search evidence. The relevance ledger shows all pairs on one page while withholding filenames,
generated captions, retrieval channels, and ranks. Rejecting a defective request clears its stale
pair judgments and requires reauthoring and repooling. The completed development relevance decision
records its actual authority as delegated AI, not creator or human ground truth: 15 relevant, 206
irrelevant, and 9 unknown judgments bind all 230 exact pairs while still authorizing no execution.

The development validator pilot includes every reviewed relevant pair, every reviewed unknown pair,
and exactly one hard negative per accepted request. Hard negatives are selected deterministically by
best retrieval rank, channel coverage, rank sum, then source hash. This produces 44 cases—15 proposed
matches, 20 proposed mismatches, and 9 proposed uncertain verdicts. Those proposals are derived
hypotheses only. The one-page verdict desk starts every case blank, withholds the proposal, exposes
all twelve RGB views and decimal measurements, and records confirmations, revisions, or rejected
cases in a separately sealed decision. A delegated-AI reviewer has now judged all 44 cases with the
same 15/20/9 distribution and every verdict carries a reason. Because the same agent performed the
relevance review, this is development evidence with explicitly non-independent provenance—not a
second independent review and not holdout evidence.

An executable fixture and thresholds may be frozen only after the development evidence supports
them. The final fixture must bind and verify every exact creator decision artifact and compare its
contents with the reviewed description, relevance, and validator answer surfaces. Editing status or
provenance fields cannot authorize execution. Until those artifacts exist and agree, `catalog
qualify run` remains blocked and no corpus metadata run may launch.

This qualification gate governs promotion of an automated bulk writer and validator. It does not
block the separate starting-catalog lane: agent-authored metadata may enter search when it binds
current measurements and renders and honestly records its producer, uncertainty, and review scope.
Such records remain authoring evidence with no compiler or qualification authority.

## Retrieval and acceptance claims

BM25, SigLIP2, DINOv3, metadata, and VLM validation are authoring evidence only.

- Basename BM25 is the minimum progressive text channel.
- Metadata BM25 adds open-vocabulary descriptions when present.
- SigLIP2 adds free-text-to-image retrieval when present.
- DINOv3 supports reference-shape retrieval and bounded substitution proposals only.
- Requirement validation checks visible candidate compatibility without changing rank.

Each result reports contributing channels and missing evidence. Mixed-enrichment
qualification is mandatory because documents do not all have identical channels. Retrieval
always has a nearest candidate, so only the frozen candidate-decision stage may conclude
that a suitable match exists or does not exist. Any relevant uncertainty produces
`evidence_insufficient`, not a false no-match.

None of these systems can emit an admitted assertion, semantic commitment, catalog entry,
or substitution set.

## Context and construction evidence

Context tests cover bounded 2–32 asset groups, exclusion of pack/directory identity,
component-versus-whole distinctions, collision companions, intentionally partial versus
visibly damaged assets, explicit abstention, and evidence closure. At least one regression
case proves that an attributed source assembly—with exact members, transforms,
parent/child relations, roles, and hashes—resolves an ambiguity that isolated renders do
not. Context output remains review evidence.

The reproducible modular pilot under `.basalt/catalog/context/modular-pilot/` binds four measured
Office components and their 12-view evidence into one request. It intentionally contains no model
receipt and records incomplete construction assessments: basename proximity and measured sockets
do not establish assembly purpose, intended mates, or compatible interfaces without attributed
assembly evidence.

Construction tests cover operation validation, explicit parameter bindings, anchors and
local frames, dependency cycles, rectangular opening decomposition, attachments, stairs,
occupancy, exact extents, deterministic hashes, and adapter lowering gaps. Fixture-specific
logic may not enter generic packages.

## Catalog-first-use evidence

`npm run eval:catalog-first-use` loads `registry/catalog.json` plus the exact retained measurement
artifact, compiles four outdoor requirements through the public `world compile` command, and
records candidate evidence, admitted selection identity, placement, and typed gaps. A second plan
changes only the outdoor-prop source-byte budget and checks that unrelated realized placements
remain stable. Until the creator admits the outdoor tranche, the honest result is four unresolved
catalog requirements and zero hand-placed fallback instances.

The same evaluation inventories sealed Roblox bindings and emits an upload packet plus a
creator-fillable binding decision. The packet separates assets required by current lowering gaps
from all admitted assets; pending sources are never upload candidates.
`eval:catalog-first-use:upload` verifies exact source bytes, derives a Roblox payload by retaining
`COLOR_0` and stripping `COLOR_1+`, and stores that payload content-addressably before publishing it
with Roblox Open Cloud under `ROBLOX_USER_ID`. It authenticates from the ignored `.env`, requires
read/write asset scope, refuses source, payload, or adaptation-policy drift and files over the API
limit, pins the expected price to zero, resumes saved operation paths, and reuses only exact
source-derived publications. The sealed upload ledger is publication evidence only.

The binding sealer verifies the upload-packet hash, exact asset, source-artifact, and upload-artifact
coverage, positive Roblox IDs, matching eligible publication-receipt hashes, and complete
Studio-observed material expectations. It inspects the retained upload payload and rejects a
binding unless those expectations cover every GLB render primitive. The connector then preflights
every distinct model's material paths and every declared placement's complete source-bounds fit
before placing any catalog instance, so one import reports the whole failing set instead of
revealing assets serially.
Offline success cannot create engine evidence; only
creator-run Studio import and readback can. The regression pilot contains affected secondary-color
sources plus unchanged controls; full admitted publication waits for the creator to confirm both.

## World observation and refinement

The observation-tower fixture builds a route-connected tower from bounded construction
operations. Its requested edit changes only the declared height and width parameters. The
controller must then:

- execute the affected compilation dependency closure and report each task actually run;
- reuse and preserve unrelated task hashes;
- rerun dependent checks and diagnostic cameras;
- prove requested dimensions, containment, occupancy/neighbor clearance, style-profile
  consistency, opening clearance, exact supported-primitive line of sight, and continuous
  route-to-platform access; and
- stop within the policy iteration budget.

Access evidence includes the ground/route entry transition, stair width, tread, riser,
landings, and headroom. Visibility uses exact intersections for supported boxes and vertical
cylinders; unsupported geometry cannot produce a conclusive clear claim.

Terrain-contact evidence binds each surface-relative route to the exact sealed terrain field
used for import and probes a bounded 5-by-5 grid over every travel span, including its interior
and full width. It proves minimum travel-top reveal and bounds exposed space beneath the
authored route body; it never treats the body's own thickness as a terrain gap. Bend radius is
a hard plan constraint; ordinary curved spans are angle-bounded and their sealed miter joins
must close the outside edge exactly. Smooth designed profiles must remain within the one-degree
pitch tessellation bound, and profile-miter evidence must bind every nonzero pitch transition
while complete bounds cover the sloped underside. Route overlaps share the lowest safe roadbed,
and junction disks bind the exact participant profiles and terrain composition. An exceeded
probe budget is `incomplete`.

The focused route-corridor fixture contains a woodland trail, cut/fill paved road, elevated
boardwalk, hillside, crest, radius-constrained bends, three-way junction, carved stream, and
coordinates on and off Roblox's 1.12-meter terrain boundaries. It proves deterministic hashes,
distinct treatments, terrain preservation beneath the boardwalk, measured supports, masks,
joint route/terrain clearance, junction grounding, and local invalidation. Run
`npm run eval:routes` to materialize its world build and Roblox import manifest beneath
`.basalt/evals/route-corridor/`.

The integrated first-region fixture is the creator-facing composition gate. Run
`npm run eval:first-finished-region` to compile one 96-meter highland preserve containing 230 real
admitted catalog instances across eleven asset types, more than 21 meters of terrain relief, seven
connected route segments, lower-ravine and summit elevated crossings, four shared junctions, a
trailhead gateway, a route-connected ridge pavilion, a cantilevered ravine overlook, and a
route-connected 13.5-meter roofed observation tower with an open stepped flight rather than
ground-filled stair wedges. It searches the current
index for optional cabin, lantern, campfire, footbridge, signpost, and tower candidates, but stores
those findings only as delegated-AI hypotheses in `remaining-gaps.json`; unreviewed candidates do
not enter the world or catalog. `requirements.json`, `spatial-plan.json`, `world-build.json`,
`roblox-import.json`, the receipt-bound Roblox binding packet, `diagnostics.json`, and `status.json`
bind the exact offline handoff under
`.basalt/evals/first-finished-region/`; four lightweight views in `diagnostics/` expose the route
and destination hierarchy without substituting for engine presentation.

Offline eligibility requires deterministic compilation, zero required catalog gaps, eligible
route and construction artifacts, exact verified Roblox bindings, and zero lowering gaps. The
destination hierarchy and traversal remain pending until the creator imports this one integrated
build, follows the directional ascent and both elevated spans, visits the tower, pavilion, and
ravine overlook, inspects catalog appearance, and preserves exact connector readback. The isolated
tower and route fixtures remain focused
regressions rather than separate creator workflows. This region does not require Modal, a metadata
worker, embeddings, or neural generation, and it makes no 40-square-kilometer completion claim.

`npm run eval:harvest-valley` materializes a separate 112-meter exterior scene from 58 catalog
models, thirteen grounded route segments and six junctions. Its regression checks deterministic

Asset binding capture proves only properties observed during a creator-run Studio operation.
The region's `status.json` keeps compiler eligibility, Roblox lowering and the creator gate
separate; the WebGL screenshots never establish `engine_verified`.

The layered planting comparison retains five identical cameras and all three inspected builds
under `.basalt/evals/harvest-valley-comparison/`: the 873-instance baseline, a sparse-ridge
development attempt, and the final 1,552-instance build. The final world hash is
`d7ece6a4d4df825030285d7f29d361de689f0a7dd928f37c28ea9d32ab76d6f4`.

heights and route travel geometry are unchanged. Offline occupancy and all thirteen route

Same-agent image inspection finds more continuous woodland undergrowth and preserved orchard

The Studio gate additionally checks realized route parts and the junction against Roblox's
actual smooth-terrain surface. The adapter coordinates bounded station and shared-junction
residuals before creation, then raycasts the entire realized footprint for intrusion and
unintended voids. An allowed voxel residual is closed by explicit grounding geometry whose
bottom contact is also raycast and whose transform and properties are read back exactly. Trace
events report the authored grounding tolerance, half-voxel realization allowance, observation
tolerance, and effective maximum independently so engine quantization cannot silently weaken
the compiler's exact-terrain requirement.
Retaining a route marker or expected hash is insufficient.

Connector regressions cover interior corrections retained across junction passes, four-way
junctions with float32 heights, physical correction limits after shared-endpoint overrides,
and rejection of regressing or divergent solvers. The accepted frames are retained directly;
the connector does not rerun them from an uncorrected baseline. Failed coordination retains
the latest resolved footprint observations in the incomplete report. Catalog geometry is
explicitly anchored before placement, and readback rejects unanchored parts. A subprocess
test exercises terminal hangup through the CLI and verifies that its import lock is released.
Multipart placement bounds union the separately rotated measured part boxes; the compiler
and connector regressions cover the oblique wind pump whose aggregate source box previously
overstated its realized footprint. Bounds failures retain expected and observed sizes before
the diagnostic truncation limit.

Every observation carries approach, platform, and overview render descriptors tied to the
exact world state and camera. The lightweight renderer is diagnostic evidence, not a visual
quality oracle. Geometric eligibility and visual assessment are separate; the offline
tower leaves visual status `unassessed`.

`npm run eval:tower` materializes both builds, the refinement and execution traces,
diagnostic render payloads, and the Roblox import manifest beneath
`.basalt/evals/observation-tower/`.

## Roblox readback

Offline lowering tests prove canonical transforms, material bindings, expected element
geometry, opening volumes, rollback behavior, authentication, and protocol parsing. Creator
readback must inspect actual engine instances and properties. For constructed output it
recomputes primitive type, center, size, material, anchored/collision state, and the parts
occupying each opening volume. For routes it checks each segment's actual frame, extent,
properties, top reveal, underside gap, and grounding contact. Every bridge run persists a
content-hashed `.basalt/roblox/import-report.json` containing all acknowledged stages, full
diagnostics, and bounded terrain extrema; this report is diagnostic evidence and is excluded
from the observed-world-state hash. Merely echoing expected hash attributes is insufficient.

The creator performs the final Studio import, play, access, view, visual, and readback gate.
Only that evidence may support `engine_verified`.

## Timed regional authoring development pilot

`scripts/generation-benchmark.mjs` measures an active agent using the existing compiler and
preview tools against the five briefs in `test/fixtures/generation-briefs.json`. It follows the
per-sample timing, error and retry evidence pattern in [Inspect's evaluation logs](https://inspect.aisi.org.uk/eval-logs.html);
it does not integrate Inspect or implement a model runtime. The Basalt-specific checks bind
reviews to compiled world and captured image hashes. Code, prepared catalog, measurements and
reference plan hashes are frozen before starting. A changed frozen input rejects further
execution. Setup is excluded and was not separately timed in the initial pilot.

The target is 300 seconds to an agent-accepted preview. Cases have a 600-second stop budget,
three compiler attempts, and population floors of 500 instances and 20 source models in a
112-meter region. Floors bound scope; they do not establish comparable quality. The clock
includes authoring, tool overhead, compilation, preview inspection and revisions. Compiler
and capture durations use a monotonic clock. Unknown model-call and total-tool counts remain
null. An eligible review requires every predeclared visual criterion to pass against the
latest successful capture; uncertain evidence cannot pass. Failed attempts remain recorded.

The initial pilot used the same agent to author briefs, plans and reviews, sequentially in
shared context. It is development evidence, not an autonomous-agent evaluation, independent
quality judgment or held-out test. Asset admission and Studio import were excluded. The plans
reused the showcase's catalog requirements and contract skeleton; their simpler layouts do
not establish a speedup at equivalent showcase quality.

## Vegetation distribution comparison

`scripts/vegetation-comparison.mjs` compares a retained scene with three predeclared seeds
and all four supplied cameras. Terrain, routes, non-plant placements, palette and per-species
instance caps are identical within each seed. The report retains all nine worlds, exact plans,
source snapshots, measured packing geometry, replay hashes, occupancy observations and 36
image receipts. The main artifact is `.basalt/evals/vegetation-comparison/index.html`.

The current condition fills independent scatter quotas using conservative visual boxes.
The adaptive condition adds bounded variable spacing, measured packing, grounding probes
and clearings. The layered condition adds open/covered preferences based on accepted trees;
its canopy is identical to the adaptive condition. Consequently only adaptive-to-layered
isolates canopy preference. This is a density-budget comparison, not a matched-count test.

All nine final compilations and their retained occupancy checks are eligible and replay
identically. Terrain, route and static-placement hashes match within each seed. The three
layered compilations take 3.66–3.78 seconds, excluding rendering and the 0.19-second one-time
palette measurement. This is compiler execution time, not prompt-to-accepted-world latency.

## Regression discipline

Every important failure should become one of:

- a schema or static boundary test;
- a deterministic unit or property test;
- a fixed smoke or qualification case;
- a replayable compilation/observation trace; or
- an engine readback assertion.

Evaluation fixtures consume generic packages. Production packages never import fixture
answers or special-case showcase prompts.
